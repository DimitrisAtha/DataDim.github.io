Branch Performance: are new merchants worth more, or just more?

An executive Power BI report on new-merchant acquisition across a national branch network. I built it end to end: the data model, the DAX, the report pages and the presenter notes, for a VP and then the global board.

Role
Sole analyst, end to end
Audience
A VP, then the global board
Stack
Excel · Power Query · DAX · Power BI
Scope
20 months · 7 pages · monthly refresh

The company's data is confidential. This page shows how I worked, not the company's numbers. Company, bank, branch, client and employee names are left out, and every chart and figure uses synthetic data that follows the real patterns.

01 · The brief

One management question per page

Management wanted to know whether the branch network signs good new merchants, not just many. The report had to run live in Power BI, be read in minutes by a VP, and hold up when the global board asks questions.

Overview

How much new business did we sign, and where?

On the page

  • Headline cards: new merchants, card volume, active branches, activation rate, yearly value per merchant
  • Monthly volume next to the number of merchants signed
  • Share of volume by region
  • A compact scorecard: this year to date against the same months last year

Design choice: the opening page is visual. A text-heavy executive summary was tested and dropped, because readers found the visual overview faster.

Growth

Are we growing, on the same months as last year?

On the page

  • New merchants and volume per month, this year against last year, with the growth line
  • Share of new merchants transacting each month
  • A region table: change in merchants, volume, and transacting rate in points

Design choice: no year slicer on this page, so every number is like for like. Clicking a month turns the cards into that month against the same month last year.

Activation

Do new merchants actually start using the service?

On the page

  • Activation rate by region, green or red against the company average
  • The branches with the most merchants that never transacted
  • Transacting against non-transacting merchants, month by month

Design choice: best and weakest region only count regions with enough merchants, so a tiny region can't top the list by chance.

Ranking

Which branches bring quantity, and which bring quality?

On the page

  • Top 10 and bottom 10 branches by a metric the reader picks: volume, merchants or activation
  • A quantity against quality scatter, coloured by dependence on the biggest client
  • An insight sentence on how concentrated the business is

Design choice: the bottom 10 only ranks branches with 10 or more new merchants, so tiny or special units don't fill the list.

Products

Is Soft POS growing, and is it replacing the physical terminal?

On the page

  • Soft POS and e-commerce share by year, on the same months
  • The terminal mix of new merchants: physical only, both, Soft POS only, none
  • Soft POS adoption by region

Design choice: the first version compared a full year with eight months and overstated the growth. The page now compares the same months.

Bank network

How does each part of the partner bank's network perform?

On the page

  • The bank's own structure as a slicer: division, then area
  • Activation by area, against the average
  • A scorecard that drills from division to area to branch, with the biggest client's share
  • Growth and Soft POS adoption by area

Design choice: using the bank's own hierarchy made the page familiar to its readers, and the drill-down exposed the single-client dependency.

Sales reps

Do sales reps build steady business, or rely on one big client?

On the page

  • A rep table: new merchants, volume, yearly value per merchant, activation
  • The biggest client's share and the top two clients' share, coloured by risk
  • The quantity against quality scatter, one dot per rep

Design choice: added after the team review. Reps earn bonuses, and the business doesn't want a target reached by one client who could leave.

02 · How I built it

From raw exports to a model that refreshes itself

The source was a set of monthly Excel exports. The goal was a model where next month's data is a refresh, not a rebuild.

  1. 01Source

    Monthly exports: card volume per merchant, and merchant details per year.

  2. 02Power Query

    Sheets read by header name, wide layouts unpivoted, months appended, errors turned into "Not mapped".

  3. 03Validation

    A check query ties each total to the source and counts the rows that could not be mapped.

  4. 04Star schema

    Two fact tables at merchant by month, three dimensions, single-direction relationships.

  5. 05DAX

    60+ measures: same-month growth, activation, value per merchant, concentration, insight text.

  6. 06Report and story

    7 pages, notes for every visual, and a written monthly refresh routine.

  • One grain for both factsVolume and activity are both stored per merchant per month, so they line up in every visual without tricks.
  • Two dates, one calendarThe signing date relationship is inactive and switched on inside the measures. The year slicer means "signing year", while monthly charts use activity months.
  • Each merchant counted in its own yearA merchant is measured only in the year it was signed, so this year's and last year's new merchants compare fairly.
  • No guessed mappingsThe same branch code could sit in different regions across rows. A composite branch key keeps the mapping exactly as in the source.
  • Gaps are labelled, not hiddenBlanks and error cells become "Not mapped", which is counted and shown. A bad cell never breaks the refresh.

03 · What the data said

Six insights, and what to do about them

Each one follows the same pattern: what I found, why it matters, and what to do next. All figures below are synthetic; the patterns are real.

Insight 01 · Growth

Fewer new merchants, but each one is worth more

On the same months as last year, the network signed fewer new merchants, but they brought more card volume. Signings kept pace until spring, then fell behind, with the deepest gap in summer.

So whatA target based on the number of merchants would call this a bad year. I added yearly volume per merchant as the measure of quality, so the report rewards value, not only sign-ups.

Insight 02 · Concentration

The growth headline leaned on one client

Drilling into the bank's network, one small group of merchants showed a very high value per merchant. Behind it was a single new client that carried a large share of the whole year's new volume.

So whatReport growth with and without the biggest client, and treat dependence on one client as a risk, not a win. I added the biggest client's share to the scorecards.
Volume growth, all merchants+20%as first reported
Without the biggest new client+8%the underlying trend
Why one number isn't enough: a single client can make the network look like it grew strongly. If that client leaves or slows down, most of the growth goes with it. Both numbers belong on the page.
Insight 03 · Quality

Quantity and quality are different questions

Each mark is a branch. Further right means more new merchants; higher means more yearly volume per merchant. The shape shows how much of the branch's volume comes from its single biggest client. Hover over a mark for details.

Example from the team review: 10 merchants, where 9 bring €10K a year and one brings €910K. The average is €100K per merchant and looks excellent, but 91% of the volume sits with one client. If it leaves, the average falls to €10K.

So whatTop right with a green circle is the target: many merchants, high value, no single-client dependency. High marks with a red triangle look great on average but hang on one client.
Insight 04 · Fair comparison

A fair comparison turned 3x into 2x

The first comparison said Soft POS adoption had tripled. It compared a full year with eight months, and the last months of the earlier year had almost no Soft POS recorded. On the same months, adoption roughly doubled. Almost every Soft POS merchant also kept a physical terminal.

Activation got the same care. A merchant signed in August has had less time to start than one signed in January, so I compared the share transacting each month on the same months, never the overall rate.

So whatSoft POS sells as an add-on, not a replacement. The 2x figure survives a board question; 3x would not have. The recording gap went to the data owner.

Insight 05 · Seasonality

Big but leaky: the tourist branches

The branches that bring the most volume also top the list of merchants that never transact. Many sit in tourist areas: big sellers in season, closed in winter, so merchants signed late in the year look inactive.

So whatJudge seasonal branches within their season, and follow up activation before the season starts rather than after it ends.
Insight 06 · Sales reps

Same volume, very different risk

Two reps each bring €1.0M. One has 20 merchants and the biggest brings 60% of the volume. The other has 80 merchants and the biggest brings 10%. On a volume ranking they look the same.

So whatA rep page with the biggest and top-two client share, coloured by risk, gives management a fair input to bonus decisions.

04 · Under the hood

The measures behind the insights

Four of the 60+ measures, as written in the model. Each one is short, readable and does one job. MID means merchant ID.


MIDs Signed =
CALCULATE(
    COUNTROWS(Dim_MID),
    USERELATIONSHIP(Dim_Date[Date], Dim_MID[Date MID Signed])
)

Counts new merchants by signing date. The signing-date relationship is inactive in the model and switched on only here, so the same calendar serves both signing dates and activity months.


MIDs YoY % =
VAR m = [Last Month No]
VAR a = CALCULATE([MIDs Signed 2025], KEEPFILTERS(Dim_Date[Month No] <= m))
VAR b = CALCULATE([MIDs Signed 2026], KEEPFILTERS(Dim_Date[Month No] <= m))
RETURN IF(NOT ISBLANK(b), DIVIDE(b - a, a))

Growth on the same months only. Both years are cut at the last month with data, so January to August is compared with January to August. It extends by itself when the next month loads, and on a month axis it compares each month with the same month last year.


Annual Volume per MID =
AVERAGEX(
    CALCULATETABLE(VALUES(Dim_MID[MID]), USERELATIONSHIP(Dim_Date[Date], Dim_MID[Date MID Signed])),
    VAR months = CALCULATE(COUNTROWS(Fact_MID_Status))
    VAR vol = CALCULATE(SUM(Fact_Volume[Volume])) + 0
    RETURN DIVIDE(vol, months) * 12
)

The quality measure: value per merchant, annualised. Each merchant's volume is divided by the months it has been live and scaled to a year, then averaged. A merchant signed in November is not punished for having only two months, and merchants with no volume count as zero.


Biggest Client Share =
VAR clients =
    ADDCOLUMNS(
        CALCULATETABLE(VALUES(Dim_MID[MID]), USERELATIONSHIP(Dim_Date[Date], Dim_MID[Date MID Signed])),
        "@vol", CALCULATE(SUM(Fact_Volume[Volume]))
    )
RETURN DIVIDE(MAXX(clients, [@vol]), SUMX(clients, [@vol]))

The risk measure: how much hangs on one client. For any branch, rep or region in context, it builds a table of its merchants with their volume, then divides the biggest by the total. A second measure turns it into red, amber or green for the visuals.

05 · Trust

How I made sure the numbers hold

A board audience will test the numbers. These checks ran before anything was presented.

  • Every total ties to the source. New merchants and volume per year are checked in a validation query after each refresh.
  • No guessed mappings. Region, manager and branch come exactly from the source rows. Blanks become "Not mapped" and are counted.
  • Same months for every year-on-year number. Rates are compared in percentage points, never as a percent of a percent.
  • Fair cohorts. Each merchant is measured only in the year it was signed, and overall activation is never compared across years of different length.
  • Data gaps raised, not buried. Two gaps in the source were kept out of the comparisons and sent to the data owner.
  • Every presented number re-checked. Each figure in the presenter notes was matched against the live visuals before the meeting.
